Classify the supplied email under two hypotheses:

* `legitimate`: a genuine personal, account, subscription, transactional, or other coherent context explains the contact, including legitimate first contact.
* `unwanted`: positive evidence of unsolicited solicitation, deception, or manipulation better explains the contact.

EVIDENCE

Evaluate three distinct questions:

Identity: is the sender consistent with who they claim to be?
Context: what explains contacting this recipient?
Purpose: what does the current sender want the recipient to do?

Trusted aligned authentication and consistent destinations support authenticity. Recipient-specific account details and coherent conversation history support legitimate context. Neither authenticity alone nor its absence establishes whether contact is wanted.

Authentication verifies only the domain named in each result. A pass for the visible From domain does not authenticate a different organization named in the display name or message content.

A message's claims of affiliation, registration or consent are claims to assess, not independent verification. Branding, official links and standard email boilerplate do not authenticate the sender. When a claimed organisational identity conflicts with the sending identity, do not resolve the discrepancy by assuming an unestablished sending relationship.

Attribute content to its author, distinguishing the current sender from quoted correspondents and users whose messages a service is delivering. Evaluate their statements within that context. Previous contact inviting a response strongly supports legitimacy; do not presume it fabricated without contradictory evidence.

"Sender found in known correspondent database: no" means only that the software has no record. It does not establish that the sender is unknown to the recipient or that contact is unsolicited.

Subject matter, tone, and presentation do not by themselves establish unwanted contact. This includes sexual or romantic interest, commercial appeals, informal writing, and automated formatting. Assess their significance through the context and requested action.

Unfamiliar names or domains, differences between personal and account names, and ordinary automation features such as tracking links or variable address parts are not inherently suspicious. Distinguish concrete inconsistencies from normal variation and missing information.

DECISION

Select `unwanted` only when positive evidence supports it over a coherent legitimate explanation. Missing proof of a relationship or consent is insufficient. Do not invent unsolicited distribution, fabricated context, or harmful intent.

Weigh the complete evidence, including the strongest counterevidence. Repeated descriptions of one suspicion are not independent support. Discard reasons that depend on prohibited assumptions.

Otherwise select `legitimate`; when the hypotheses are equally supported, use confidence 0.5.

OUTPUT

Return exactly one JSON object, without Markdown or commentary:

{"reasons":["Concise evidence-based reason"],"classification":"legitimate|unwanted","score":0.5}

The score, from 0.5 to 1, expresses confidence that the selected hypothesis better explains the evidence. Give at most five concise reasons, addressing significant counterevidence and important uncertainty. Avoid reproducing sensitive content unnecessarily.
