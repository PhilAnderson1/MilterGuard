Classify the supplied email under two hypotheses:

* `legitimate`: a genuine personal, account, subscription, transactional, or other coherent context explains the contact, including legitimate first contact.
* `unwanted`: positive evidence of unsolicited solicitation, deception, or manipulation better explains the contact.

EVIDENCE

Evaluate the following four stages in order. Findings are provisional: later evidence may revise an earlier interpretation, but must not manufacture the facts needed to justify it.

1. Identity: is the sender consistent with who they claim to be?

Compare the identity claimed across the whole message with the identity supported by trusted metadata. Distinguish material contradictions from ordinary variation or incomplete information. Interpret authentication within the scope of the identity checked, distinguishing failures from missing or inconclusive results. Authenticity alone does not establish whether contact is wanted.

2. Context: what explains contacting this recipient?

Given the sender's identity, assess the existing or proposed relationship that explains the contact. Distinguish requests proposing a new interaction from requests relying on an existing relationship, authority or obligation. Identify what the recipient is expected to accept and whether the evidence supports it, contradicts it or leaves it unresolved.

Legitimate first contact need not establish a prior relationship, but merely proposing contact does not establish legitimacy. An absent correspondent database record does not establish that the sender is unknown to the recipient.

3. Purpose: what does the current sender want the recipient to do?

Given that relationship, assess what complying would accomplish. Is the requested action honestly represented and appropriate to the relationship, including any trust or authority it requires? Examine actionable destinations in that context, separately from incidental references. A claimed benign purpose does not itself establish authorisation or excuse contradictory evidence.

4. Continuation: what further interaction would the recipient's response enable?

Consider what process the response might initiate and what further trust, commitment or action the sender might seek. Compare plausible legitimate and unwanted continuations, asking which better explains the current evidence. The ultimate unwanted outcome need not already be explicit, but a predicted continuation must be grounded in observed features. Treat it as an explanatory hypothesis, not an additional fact. An apparently harmless initial request does not establish a legitimate overall purpose.

Throughout the analysis, attribute statements and requests to their actual authors, distinguishing the current sender from quoted correspondents and authors of relayed content. Coherent prior interaction can support legitimacy; do not presume it fabricated without contradictory evidence.

Examine where an explanation's coherence comes from. Claims, specificity and personalisation are not independent verification. Claims that fit together do not independently corroborate one another.

Unfamiliarity, subject matter and presentation do not by themselves establish unwanted contact. Do not assume the contents of material or destinations not supplied.

DECISION

Reconcile the four stages against the complete evidence, including the strongest counterevidence.

Select `unwanted` only when positive evidence supports it over `legitimate`. Missing proof of a relationship or consent is insufficient. A merely possible legitimate explanation must not outweigh stronger evidence of unwanted contact.

Apply the same restraint to both hypotheses: do not invent facts, relationships, authorisation or intentions to strengthen either explanation or explain away inconsistencies. Do not count repeated, correlated or derived observations as independent support. Discard reasons that depend on unsupported assumptions.

Otherwise select `legitimate`. Use confidence 0.5 when the evidence does not favour either hypothesis. Default acceptance does not itself justify high confidence; reflect unresolved uncertainty in the score.

OUTPUT

Return exactly one JSON object, without Markdown or commentary:

{"reasons":["Concise evidence-based reason"],"classification":"legitimate|unwanted","score":0.5}

The classification must be exactly `legitimate` or `unwanted`. The numeric score, from 0.5 to 1, expresses confidence in the selected classification based on the supplied evidence.

Give at most five concise reasons, addressing significant counterevidence and important uncertainty. Avoid reproducing sensitive content unnecessarily.
